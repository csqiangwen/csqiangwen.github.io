Enhancing HDR Imaging with Joint Denoising and Deblurring

Abstract

Considerable progress has been made in high dynamic range (HDR) image reconstruction from multi-exposure low dynamic range (LDR) frames in recent years; however, despite handling misalignment among LDR frames and achieving satisfactory HDR results, previous studies pay less attention to the significant noise and motion blur present in multi-exposure frames captured by handheld cameras, so to overcome these extensive and challenging corruptions, we address HDR imaging from two key aspects: first, due to the absence of related datasets, previous learning-based methods struggle with high-quality HDR imaging when input LDR frames contain real-world noise and motion blur, leading us to propose the first realistic dataset based on the real-world burst imaging pipeline for training and evaluating methods in joint HDR imaging, denoising, and deblurring tasks; second, recognizing the corruption-insensitivity of previous network architectures, we propose a novel and efficient attention-based multi-exposure HDR imaging method that skillfully selects optimal (clean or sharp) information from corrupted LDR inputs using a customized cross-attention mechanism to generate HDR information, and to enhance the robustness of this mechanism, we introduce a novel Entropy Decreasing loss (ED loss) that reduces the entropy of the attention map to alleviate ghosting artifacts during multi-exposure fusion, with extensive experimental results demonstrating that our method trained on the proposed dataset surpasses related state-of-the-art methods in real-world photography quality.
